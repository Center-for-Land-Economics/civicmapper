Draw

Polygon: click each corner, then click the first corner (or press Enter) to close. Rectangle: click two corners along one edge, then click to set the width. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, Esc cancels a shape in progress. Edit: click a shape (the smallest under the pointer; click again to reach the one beneath), drag its corners, then change its class or delete it. Paint: drag to paint pixels with the current class (cut-out paint clears); right- or Alt-drag erases, Space+drag pans, [ ] brush size. Paint overrides shapes. From parcel: click inside a dotted parcel to copy it as a shape. Snap pulls corners onto shapes, parcels and the square edge; hold Alt to place freely.

Labelling guidelines

Outline every ground-level surface used to park or store vehicles inside the bright square. Shapes may run past the square's edge; only the part inside counts.

Paved parking 1 — draw it

  • Asphalt or concrete lots, including their drive aisles and the striped stalls.
  • Car-dealership inventory lots, truck and trailer yards, bus and fleet depots, rental-car lots.
  • Parking on churches, schools, government and other tax-exempt land: label it the same. Tax status is handled separately.
  • Apartment and townhome parking courts. Large paved areas at houses (more than a normal driveway).

Gravel, caliche or dirt lots 2

  • Unpaved ground clearly used for parking or vehicle storage: tracks, rows of vehicles, worn surface.

Garages and rooftop parking 3

  • Structured garages and roof-deck parking are not surface parking. Outline them as class 3 so the model learns to leave them out.

Do not draw

  • Public streets, alleys, highway ramps, ordinary single-family driveways.
  • Loading docks only big enough for a truck at a door, sidewalks, plazas, sports courts, building roofs.
  • Open storage of materials (pipe, pallets, containers) with no vehicles, unless it is also a vehicle yard.

How to draw

  • Follow the pavement edge. Include drive aisles inside the lot; stop at the street curb or property driveway.
  • Buildings with a yellow outline (Overture, key O) are removed from parking automatically: draw the lot straight across them. Cut out only buildings, kiosks or sheds with no yellow outline.
  • Landscaped islands bigger than about 3 parking spaces: draw the whole lot first, then draw a Cut-out (5 or X) over each island. Cut-outs show dark and are subtracted from the lot when labels are exported. Use them for a kiosk or building inside a lot too.
  • Trees over a lot: draw the lot where you can infer it. Tick Tree cover if you had to guess.
  • Shadows: late-season imagery (e.g. November) has long building shadows. A shadowed lot is still a lot.
  • Use Infrared (C) to separate grass and trees (bright red) from pavement (grey/blue-green).
  • Use Unsure (4) for a shape you think is parking but can't confirm.
  • No parking (N) when the square truly has none. Can't tell (T) when imagery is unusable. Skip (K) to pass the square to someone else.

Modes

  • Blind: draw from scratch. Used to measure what existing map data misses.
  • Correction: existing OpenStreetMap/Overture lots are pre-loaded as paved shapes; fix, delete or add.
